\section{Conclusion}


The goal of this thesis was to analyze whether Monte Carlo PageRank is a viable alternative to standard PageRank methods, such as the GraphX PageRank implementation. The central research question was whether Monte Carlo PageRank can operate under constrained memory conditions while sacrificing only a little accuracy and performance. Evaluations across real-world and synthetic graphs prove that the approximate Monte Carlo method outperforms standard iterative methods.\par

One of the key findings is that, across all graph sizes and structures, Monte Carlo PageRank successfully operates on only 450 MiB per executor, while GraphX fails on low memory configurations and suffers from low performance, even with the highest memory configuration of 8 GiB. Surprisingly, the low memory configuration does not compromise performance or accuracy, as was initially expected. On web graphs, Monte Carlo PageRank maintains a consistent runtime of approximately 100 seconds, whereas GraphX experiences notable performance issues with low memory configurations. Additionally, the approximate approach achieves 75\%-90\% similarity, depending on the walker configuration, when compared to the top 20 nodes of GraphX PageRank. Considering that GraphX cannot operate successfully across all memory configurations, achieving an approximate ranking is an acceptable compromise. Moreover, cost experiments show that Monte Carlo is less costly than GraphX's PageRank, using five to ten times fewer resources. The performance behavior of Monte Carlo PageRank also makes the method much more predictable.\par

However, Monte Carlo PageRank is not a universal solution. Applications that require exact rankings should rely on iterative methods that guarantee precise results. Another drawback is the inconsistent results across experiments due to the randomness of Monte Carlo PageRank. Therefore, multiple runs are required to obtain a stable ranking. Although the approximate method performs better in environments with memory constraints, GraphX may have lower runtimes when sufficient memory is allocated.\par

Future work could further evaluate the Monte Carlo method on synthetic graphs of different sizes based on the number of edges rather than nodes. Additionally, analyzing the behavior of Monte Carlo PageRank on dynamic graphs could be of interest. Finally, a system could be developed that decides which method is best for a given graph and requirements. \par

This thesis demonstrates that the approximate Monte Carlo PageRank method is a more memory- and cost-efficient alternative to standard iterative PageRank methods.
